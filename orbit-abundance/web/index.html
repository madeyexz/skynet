Today a free satellite looks 3× a week. What if it looked every 10 minutes?

The first half of this page runs on real Copernicus Sentinel-1 radar and Sentinel-2 imagery over eastern Libya and the Crete approaches, pulled for the last 60 days. 05How fast each world looks

What changes, and what does not
  • Revisit collapses. Real Sentinel-1 data: 2.2–3.4 looks per week at each point, with blind gaps of up to 144 h. Model: 100 agile SAR satellites give a look every ~16 min, and 1,000 give one every ~1.6 min.
  • Resolution decides what you can see. Sentinel-1 recall drops "steeply" below 25 m (Paolo et al., Nature 2024), so a 10 m inflatable is nearly invisible to it. At 3 m resolution the same boat spans several pixels.
  • Swath decides whether you are looking in the right place. A 0.5 m optical scene covers 100 km², which is 0.3% of the 30,000 km² search box. Abundance helps sharp, narrow sensors least, unless they are cued by a wide-area sensor.
  • Price now sets the limit. Tasking every pass of a 1,000-satellite Strip constellation over this corridor costs about $1.2M a day at today's list price. With a fixed budget the curve flattens until price falls, which is why the model has budget and price controls.
  • Still impossible from orbit: knowing who is on board, telling a migrant trawler from a fishing trawler without AIS or a second look, and seeing inside a harbour before boarding.
